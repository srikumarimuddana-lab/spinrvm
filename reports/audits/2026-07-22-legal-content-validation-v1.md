# Legal Content — Privacy Policy, Terms of Service & Breach Register (Round 1)

**Date:** 2026-07-22
**Scope:** Item #4 of the broader multi-surface review requested — "Build Privacy policy and terms and condition (validate old and enhance) and any other txt content needed to be compliant to regulation, provincial and federal by-laws."
**Method:** Two code-grounded research passes (how the legal-content system actually works end-to-end; a full compliance-grounding sweep across `docs/data-classification.md`, `docs/dpa-register.md`, `docs/vendor-inventory.md`/`vendor-register.md`, `docs/incident-response.md`, `docs/runbooks/data-breach.md`, `docs/accessibility-plan.md`/`docs/ACCESSIBILITY.md`, `reports/legal/supabase-region-attestation-checklist.md`, `docs/audit/admin-dashboard/05-privacy-logging.md`), then drafting.
**Deliverables:** `docs/legal/terms-of-service.md`, `docs/legal/privacy-policy.md`, `docs/audit/breach-record.md` (closes `ACTION_ITEMS.md` A3).
**Standard caveat:** everything drafted here is grounded in this repo's actual code and documented data practices, not generic boilerplate — but it has not been reviewed by a lawyer licensed in Saskatchewan or Canada. Treat it as a strong first draft for counsel to review, not publication-ready text.

## 1. The most important finding: validating "the old" content surfaced a live product bug

You asked to validate the existing privacy policy and terms of service before enhancing them. There is no single "old" document to validate — there are three different pieces of legal text living in three different places, and **only one of them is what users actually see today**:

| Text | Where it lives | Reaches real users? |
|---|---|---|
| Dev placeholder ("This text is a development placeholder...") | `backend/routes/settings.py`, served whenever the admin-editable blob is empty | Yes, by default, until an admin fills in the real Settings → Terms/Privacy textareas |
| **Hardcoded fallback text baked into the driver-app binary** | `driver-app/app/legal.tsx`, used only if the network fetch fails | Yes, on network failure, and **cannot be fixed by publishing new policy text** — it ships inside the compiled app |
| The newer, better-designed per-audience versioned table (`legal_documents`) | Admin dashboard → Support → Legal Documents tab | **No** — neither app reads this table today |

**The driver-app's hardcoded fallback text states: "These Terms are governed by the laws of the Province of Ontario, Canada."** Spinr's actual, documented operating jurisdiction is Saskatchewan (per `CLAUDE.md`, `docs/runbooks/saskatoon-launch.md`, and every regulatory reference in this codebase). This is a real, live, factually-wrong governing-law clause sitting in the app you'd ship to the Play Store / App Store today, and it is invisible to anyone editing content through the admin dashboard, because it only appears when the live fetch fails — publishing new policy text through Settings does not touch it. **This needs a code change, not a content change** — flagged as LGL-1 below.

## 2. The second most important finding: the newer legal system is disconnected from the apps

Someone already built a better system than what's live: `legal_documents` is a real Postgres table with per-audience (rider/driver) and per-doc-type (ToS/privacy) rows, versioned, with a dedicated admin editor (`admin-dashboard/src/app/dashboard/support/_tabs/legal-documents.tsx`) and a working public read endpoint (`GET /legal-documents?audience=&type=`). **Nothing in `rider-app` or `driver-app` calls that endpoint.** Both apps still call the older `GET /settings/legal`, which returns one shared blob for both audiences, edited from a completely different Settings-tab textarea with no versioning at all.

Practically, this means: if someone pastes drafted rider-specific and driver-specific content into the "Legal Documents" tab expecting it to go live, **it will save correctly but never render in either app.** This is flagged as LGL-2 below — it's a small, well-scoped engineering fix (point both apps' `legal.tsx` fetch calls at the newer endpoint), but until it ships, the two documents in this deliverable are deliberately written as **one combined document each** (Part A for everyone, Part B for drivers) so they work correctly through the live `/settings/legal` path today, with a note on how to split them once the wiring is fixed.

## 3. What's delivered

### `docs/legal/terms-of-service.md`
Combined Terms of Service — Part A (rider/general), Part B (driver-specific: independent-contractor status, eligibility, insurance periods, accessibility/WAV obligations). Grounded in the actual fare/surge/cancellation/SOS/insurance-period behavior documented in `CLAUDE.md` and confirmed by the driver-tracking audit (`reports/audits/2026-07-22-scheduled-rides-driver-tracking-audit-v1.md`) earlier in this review. Governing law correctly stated as Saskatchewan.

### `docs/legal/privacy-policy.md`
Combined Privacy Policy — what's collected, why, who it's shared with (full vendor list below), retention periods, your rights (mapped to the actual in-app controls: "Download My Data," "Delete Account," marketing-preference toggles — confirmed these are the only real controls that exist, per the legal-content-system research pass), breach notification commitment, accessibility statement. **This draft is what closes `docs/data-classification.md`'s open item DV-16** (Google Gemini was not disclosed as a sub-processor) — Gemini is now named in the vendor list, along with LogRocket, which was also missing from the compliance register per the earlier implementation-plan audit.

### `docs/audit/breach-record.md`
Closes `ACTION_ITEMS.md` **A3** (explicitly listed as the "single biggest remaining gap" in the P0 launch-gating section alongside A1). Uses the fuller field set from `docs/runbooks/data-breach.md`'s breach-record template (discovery/start/end timestamps, data categories, RROSH determination, OPC notification, SGI notification where applicable, affected-user notification, root cause, fix, post-mortem link, sign-off) rather than the shorter 5-column version originally sketched in `ACTION_ITEMS.md`, since the runbook's version is more complete and this file needs to match what the runbook tells responders to fill in. Also resolves a file-path inconsistency: `docs/dpa-register.md` referenced a second, different path (`reports/compliance/breach-register.md`) for the same register — this file is now the single canonical location, and `docs/dpa-register.md` should be updated to point here (see LGL-6).

## 4. Findings register

| ID | Sev | Finding | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|
| LGL-1 | **P0** | `driver-app/app/legal.tsx`'s hardcoded network-failure fallback states Spinr's Terms are governed by Ontario law — factually wrong, and unreachable/unfixable by publishing new policy content since it's baked into the app binary. | Fix in code: either delete the hardcoded fallback (show a "couldn't load — try again" state instead, consistent with how `rider-app`'s equivalent screen already handles this) or update the hardcoded text to match the real published Terms and keep it in sync going forward. Needs a mobile app release to actually reach users, not just a content edit. |
| LGL-2 | **P1** | The versioned, per-audience `legal_documents` table and its admin editor are fully built but not read by either app — both still use the older shared-blob `/settings/legal` endpoint. | Point `rider-app/app/legal.tsx` and `driver-app/app/legal.tsx` at `GET /legal-documents?audience=&type=`, with a fallback to `/settings/legal` during rollout (the backend endpoint already supports this fallback pattern in reverse — worth mirroring client-side). Once shipped, split this round's combined documents into the four per-audience rows as described in each document's closing section — no rewriting needed, just a copy/paste split. |
| LGL-3 | **P1** | No mechanism exists anywhere to track which version of the Terms/Privacy Policy a given user has accepted, or to force re-consent when the text materially changes — despite `CLAUDE.md` stating "material changes require re-consent" and `docs/runbooks/saskatoon-launch.md` listing this as an open pre-launch checklist item (R-4). A similarly-named `consent_version` column exists, but only for CASL marketing consent — an unrelated purpose. | This is a real engineering dependency, not a content problem: add a `users.tos_version`/`drivers.tos_version` column (or equivalent), compare it against the live `legal_documents.version` at login, and show a blocking re-consent screen on mismatch. Until this exists, publishing this round's documents as "v2" of anything is a labeling exercise only — no user will actually be prompted to re-accept. |
| LGL-4 | **P1** | Admin edits to `legal_documents` are overwrite-only — saving a new version destroys the previous text with no history table, only an incrementing integer. If a dispute or regulator ever asks "what did the policy say on the date this user signed up," there is no way to answer from the database alone. | Add a `legal_document_versions` (or similar) history table before this becomes load-bearing for compliance — cheap now, expensive to reconstruct later. In the meantime, this repo's own git history of `docs/legal/*.md` is the only real version record; treat every future edit to those files as the source of truth and mirror it into the admin editor deliberately. |
| LGL-5 | **P2** | `docs/data-classification.md` states the pickup/drop-off GPS retention period as **2 years** in two places; `CLAUDE.md` and `docs/runbooks/data-breach.md` both independently state **3 years**. | LGL-7 | **P2** | `docs/dpa-register.md` marks Stripe/Google/Vercel DPAs as signed (✅); the more recently-touched `docs/vendor-register.md` lists every vendor's DPA effective date as `TBD — file DPA`, including those same three. ## 5. Rolled-up open compliance items this round did not (and could not) close

These require action outside a documentation pass — engineering work, vendor contract execution, or a live-system verification — and are tracked in their source documents; listed here so nothing falls through the crack between this round and those docs:

- **DV-8** (`docs/data-classification.md`) — no scheduled hard-delete job exists yet at any of the retention horizons this policy now promises in writing. This is the single most important non-drafting follow-up: publishing a policy that promises deletion timelines nothing currently enforces is a real exposure once the policy is live, not before.
- DPA execution gaps for Supabase, Twilio, Railway, Expo/EAS, and Upstash/Redis (all "⚠ VERIFY" or "TBD" across the two vendor trackers, see LGL-7).
- Several Q2-2026-targeted items in `docs/vendor-register.md` (Sentry EU/PII-scrubbing migration, Gemini PII-stripping middleware) that are now overdue relative to today's date and need a status check, not just a citation.
- Mobile accessibility audits (`docs/ACCESSIBILITY.md` confirms neither rider-app nor driver-app has been audited yet) and the ACA-applicability legal memo (`docs/accessibility-plan.md`, still unfiled).
- Admin-dashboard privacy findings F-41 through F-47 (`docs/audit/admin-dashboard/05-privacy-logging.md`) — notably F-42 (ride-invoice PDFs embed exact 5-decimal GPS coordinates, arguably beyond data-minimization needs) and F-46 (`audit_logs` RLS still permits UPDATE/DELETE, undermining its value as a forensic/breach-investigation source) are both directly relevant to how credible the breach-response process described in this round's Privacy Policy actually is in practice.

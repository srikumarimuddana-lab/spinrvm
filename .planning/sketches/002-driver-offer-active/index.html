Design Notes
Offer Moment Psychology context
  • Driver has ~15 seconds — a cognitive peak of urgency
  • Anxiety reduces decision quality; calm confidence improves it
  • Fare shown first anchors the decision positively
  • Too much urgency → panic declines → lower accept rate
A — Timer-Dominant caution
  • Ring is visually dominant; fare is secondary
  • Stress-induces fast response but increases regret declines
  • Good for high-volume, low-value trips
  • Risk: timer anxiety with partial info → irrational declines
B — Information-First measured
  • Full transparency reduces post-accept regret
  • Fare breakdown mirrors Monzo's "radical transparency"
  • Scroll required → risk of missing timer expiry while reading
  • Best for experienced drivers who want full context
C — Cinematic ★ recommended
  • Large fare number creates immediate positive anchor
  • Pulsing rings convey urgency without stress typography
  • Key info (pickup dist, rider rating) in slide panel
  • Linear timer progress bar is calmer than a countdown ring
  • All action within thumb reach at bottom
Recommendation
Variant C creates confident urgency — the fare is the protagonist, not the timer. Drivers feel motivated by the earnings opportunity rather than pressured by a shrinking clock. This aligns with Spinr's "driver dignity" principle.
In-Trip UX Priorities context
  • Driver attention must stay on the road — minimal UI
  • Navigation is primary; fare is secondary
  • Rider contact must be 1 tap away (safety)
  • SOS must be visually present but not alarming
A — Nav-dominant topbar caution
  • Floating nav card competes with map for attention
  • Panel at bottom is good but compressed
  • SOS in topbar: easy to reach but small tap target
B — Header status + map caution
  • Red header is high-distraction — unnecessary
  • Visible SOS in header is a pro
  • Map area is constrained by fixed header
C — Map-dominant + minimal dock ★ recommended
  • Floating nav preserves full map visibility
  • Bottom dock is compact: fare + ETA + rider in one card
  • SOS stays accessible without dominating the screen
  • Single-glance design reduces cognitive load while driving
Key Insight
In-trip, less is more. The driver knows the context; they don't need it restated. The only new information is turn directions and running fare. Everything else should stay out of the way.

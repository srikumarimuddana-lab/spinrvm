A — Information-Dense

Anxiety model: More data = more control. Shows driver route steps, running fare, turn-by-turn nav strip, all simultaneously.

Risk: Information overload. Riders don't need turn-by-turn (driver drives); showing it may increase, not reduce, anxiety. Running fare meter can cause "watching the clock" stress.

Best for: Power users who want to verify route correctness, track every cent.

B — Calm Minimal

Anxiety model: Reduce visible info → reduce stress. Giant ETA countdown during wait, near-empty map during trip, celebration screen on arrival.

Risk: Under-serving riders with legitimate anxiety (safety, wrong route). No visible SOS entry point without tapping, no driver details surfaced proactively.

Best for: Regular users on familiar routes who trust the system.

C — State-Aware Narrative ★

Anxiety model: Match information density to the anxiety curve. Wait state = high info (driver narrative card, contact, safety strip). In-trip state = ambient calm (fare in corner, ETA strip, progress bar). Complete = Monzo-style receipt, rating, "driver kept 100%".

Key UX principle: The narrative card ("Alex is 2 stops away") addresses pick-up anxiety with human language, not just a countdown timer. It answers "where is Alex?" not "how many minutes?"

Safety: SOS accessible on every state — inline pill in trip, chip in safety strip during wait, never buried.

Saskatchewan compliance: Receipt shows GST (5%) + PST (6%) as separate labeled line items. "Commission to Spinr: $0.00 ✓" reinforces 0% model on every completed trip.

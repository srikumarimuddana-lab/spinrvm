Design Decision Summary
A — Floating SOS Button
Maximally discoverable — a persistent red SOS circle is hard to miss. But the constant red button on the map creates ambient anxiety during normal rides, and a single accidental tap opens the emergency modal. No protection against false triggers. Appropriate for a very high-risk use case (lone workers, medical transport) but too aggressive for a mainstream ride-share surface.
B — Safety Drawer
Calm and unobtrusive during normal rides. The bottom sheet layout provides excellent touch targets once open. The weakness is discoverability under stress — finding a small text chip labeled "Safety" while panicking is unreliable. Top-left placement is also out of the natural thumb zone on most phones. Good for secondary safety features, not the primary SOS path.
C — Shield + Hold Overlay ← Winner
The shield icon sits in the ride panel's bottom action bar — thumb-zone, always visible during a ride. One tap opens a full-screen overlay (ride continues in background). 911 is a single deliberate tap. The "Alert Contacts" hold mechanism (2s) filters out accidental triggers without slowing down genuine emergencies. Icons lead every button so the layout works without reading. "I'm Safe — Close" is always prominent to eliminate trapped-screen anxiety.

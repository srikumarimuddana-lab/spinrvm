### 2.1 Upfront guaranteed vs metered pricing

### Keep quote-locked metered pricing (fare shown at booking, HMAC token, incremental settlement) — ADOPT (keep)
- What it is (plain language): the rider sees a fare built from disclosed components (base, distance, time, booking fee, area fees, tax) before booking; an HMAC `estimate_token` locks the *shown* surge multiplier to the *charged* one; the final settlement adjusts only distance/time from the actual trip, not the whole pricing model.
- Who uses it / source: this is closer to how metered taxi and most Canadian TNC pricing historically worked than to Uber's 2021+ "Upfront Fares," which prices from trip characteristics **and** an algorithmic estimate of "what a rider is likely willing to pay" and "the lowest pay a driver is likely to accept" — a Columbia Business School study (cited via search) found Uber's revenue share of the fare rose from about 32% to over 42% between 2022 and late 2024 under this model, with trip time/distance/surge explaining only 77% of observed price and pay levels — implying the remaining ~23% is not derivable from the disclosed trip facts ([Uber Under the Hood: Understanding Upfront Fares](https://uberpubpolicy.medium.com/understanding-upfront-fares-7ab69c656101), [coverage of the Columbia study](https://len-sherman.medium.com/will-other-companies-follow-ubers-lead-into-the-black-hole-of-opaque-algorithmic-pricing-d79acd9cfe35), snippets, read 2026-09-25, INFERRED). Uber's own help page confirms the fare **can still change mid-trip** for a driver detour or heavy traffic ([Uber Help: my upfront fare was not honored](https://help.uber.com/en/riders/article/my-upfront-fare-was-not-honored?nodeId=ff65490e-2ffb-41cf-a709-4611521c7b24), snippet, INFERRED) — i.e. even "upfront" fares are not fully guaranteed against route/time changes, so the two models converge more than the marketing suggests.
- Spinr today: quote-locked metered pricing with a signed token exists and is VERIFIED sound (`money-cra.md` §3.1 row 6, §3.9); the gap is not the pricing *model*, it is that completion re-pricing does not recompute tax (MONEY-007) and that a booking fee is switched to $0 so "no cut" is only true by config (STRAT-002/-003).
- Benefit for Spinr at our scale: high — algorithmic upfront pricing needs the demand-forecasting and elasticity-modelling infrastructure Spinr does not have, and CLAUDE.md's own guardrail ("not a hidden-fee operator... every charge maps to a disclosed line item") is close to incompatible with a price built partly from willingness-to-pay rather than disclosed trip facts. Cost/effort: **none** (already built). Risk: none from keeping it.
- How it fails or gets gamed: a rider or driver who disputes "the fare changed" has no visibility into *why* beyond the itemised receipt — this is already the honest version of the failure mode; Uber's version hides the reason behind an opaque algorithm, which is strictly worse for dispute handling, not better.
- Guardrail check: **ADOPT is the disclosure-first choice** (G-disclosure) — a component-based fare a rider can audit against the receipt is what "every charge maps to a disclosed line item" requires; an algorithmic upfront price that a rider cannot decompose would not meet it even if legal.
- First step behind a flag: none needed to keep the model; the improvement is MONEY-007 (recompute tax on re-price), already ranked in ROADMAP X5(b) — cross-referenced here, not re-ranked.

### Algorithmic "upfront" pricing (price from willingness-to-pay signals, not disclosed trip facts) — HOLD
- What it is: set the rider price and driver pay independently and algorithmically, using signals beyond distance/time/surge (rider history, device, time of day elasticity).
- Who uses it / source: Uber's model since 2021, expanding in 2025 (same URLs as above, INFERRED).
- Spinr today: not present, and no code path suggests it (VERIFIED absence — `fare_service.py`'s `calculate_fare` takes only distance, time, surge and configured rates as inputs, no rider-specific term).
- Why HOLD: it directly threatens two "What Spinr Is NOT" guardrails at once — "not a hidden-fee operator" (every charge must map to a disclosed line item; a willingness-to-pay adjustment has no line item) and arguably "not a surge-first product" in spirit (an opaque per-rider multiplier that behaves like unbounded, undisclosed surge). It also needs demand-elasticity data Spinr does not have at two-city scale. Revisit only if Spinr's own leadership decides disclosure-compatible personalization is worth building — and even then, publish the model's inputs, per the CLAUDE.md guardrail.

### Driver-facing per-ride cost-stack disclosure ("this ride cost Spinr $X; you kept $Y") — TRIAL
- What it is: show the driver, per trip, what the platform actually spent (Stripe processing fee, any platform-funded absorption) alongside what they earned — turning the structural 0% into a *felt* 0%.
- Who uses it / source: Lyft ships an earnings summary that shows drivers "how each rider's payment is split between drivers, Lyft, and external fees," and committed in 2024 to a **70%-of-fare weekly floor** with a make-up payment if a driver falls below it ([Lyft: How Lyft Pricing Actually Works](https://www.lyft.com/blog/posts/lets-talk-about-lyft-pricing), [PYMNTS: Lyft guarantees 70%+](https://www.pymnts.com/news/ridesharing/2024/lyft-guarantees-rideshare-drivers-70percent-or-more-of-fare/), snippets, read 2026-09-25, INFERRED) — i.e. a *percentage-take* competitor is now marketing transparency and a floor as a driver-retention feature, which is a lower bar than Spinr's actual 0%.
- Spinr today: not built; `04-blueprint.md`'s Driver Earnings & Payouts epic already proposes exactly this (`driver_trip_receipt` view, cost-stack line) as a "Later" step gated on STRAT-002's Stripe-fee measurement existing first — this session concurs with that sequencing rather than reordering it.
- Benefit for Spinr at our scale: potentially the single most defensible "trust" feature in the whole audit, because Spinr's number is actually 100% (not 70%-and-rising like the competitor above) — but the claim in `04-blueprint.md` that this is a defensible competitive edge is explicitly labelled ASSUMED there (`08-hostile-review.md` U-5/U-6: no driver survey or competitor-disclosure-practice source was fetched). Cost/effort: **M** (needs `stripe_fee` captured per settlement first). Risk: low — it is a display feature over data Spinr already has once the fee column exists.
- How it fails or gets gamed: none directly (display-only); the risk is disclosure fatigue if shown on every screen rather than the trip receipt.
- Guardrail check: strengthens G-commission and G-disclosure simultaneously — the clearest guardrail-aligned technique in this file.
- First step behind a flag: unchanged from `04-blueprint.md` — add a `stripe_fee` column captured at settlement (STRAT-002 prerequisite), then the `driver_trip_receipt` view. Measure success by: the driver survey `08-hostile-review.md` flags as missing evidence — run one before claiming a retention effect.

### 2.2 Double-entry ledger patterns

### Immutable append-only journal with derived balances — ADOPT (destination), REJECT as a near-term "only writer" (endorsing `04-blueprint.md` H2, not re-litigating it)
- What it is (plain language): every money movement is one or more journal *entries* (debit one account, credit another, always balancing to zero); nothing is ever updated or deleted; any account's current balance is a `SUM()` over its entries, not a stored, independently-writable number.
- Who uses it / source: this is the standard pattern in fintech ledger design writing — "the key principle is that every balance is derived from immutable, append-only journal entries rather than stored as a mutable number in a database row," and "enforcing [the debit=credit invariant] at the database transaction layer rather than in application code means no partial failure can produce an unbalanced entry" ([Trio: Payment Ledger Architecture](https://trio.dev/payment-ledger-architecture-fintech/), [Fintechly: Ledger System Design](https://fintechly.com/infrastructure/infrastructure-ledger-system-design/), [SDK.finance: What Is a Double-Entry Ledger](https://sdk.finance/blog/what-is-a-double-entry-ledger-in-fintech/), snippets, read 2026-09-25, INFERRED — general fintech-writing consensus, not one named company's engineering blog, since none of Uber/Lyft/DoorDash/Grab publishes ledger-internals detail that surfaced in search).
- Spinr today: `04-blueprint.md` H2 already answered this exactly: `financial_events` (migration 58) is single-entry and append-only; `financial_event_entries` (the double-entry legs, migration 286) exist as a *derived projection*, correctly shaped, but gated by `ledger_double_entry_enabled` whose production value is **UNKNOWN** and which "assumes off" on any read error (`ledger_service.py:433`, VERIFIED). This is architecturally the right destination and is already the right shape — the gap is that it has never run at production volume.
- Benefit for Spinr at our scale: high, but only once exercised — the entire value of the pattern (an unbalanced entry is structurally impossible) is unrealised while the projection has never been on. Cost/effort: **S** to turn on in staging (the code exists); **M** to make it authoritative later. Risk: low in staging; medium once it gates a live settlement path (H2's own risk rating).
- How it fails or gets gamed: none directly — the risk is process, not the pattern: turning on `ledger_double_entry_enabled` in production before the FLOAT8 columns (§3.6) are fixed lets the projection silently drift from the money it is supposed to mirror, which is worse than not having it.
- First step behind a flag (already the plan; this session does not reorder it): flip `ledger_double_entry_enabled` in **staging only**, run the projection over historical rides, and watch `financial_event_entries_unbalanced_between` (the view `reconciliation.py` already has, §3.3 row m) go and stay green for 7 days before considering production. Measure success by: zero unbalanced-entry rows over the staging replay, and the leg-completeness check (row n) reporting 100% coverage.

### A named chart of accounts (rider receivable, platform cash, driver payable, tax payable, promo expense, dispute reserve) — TRIAL
- What it is: instead of ad hoc `event_type` strings, define a small fixed set of accounts the ledger can move money between, so every settlement is expressible as "debit X, credit Y" and a query like "how much do we owe drivers right now" becomes one account balance instead of a 4-table join.
- Who uses it / source: standard double-entry accounting practice; the same fintech-design sources above describe "chart of accounts" as the schema-design step that makes the invariant meaningful (INFERRED, general accounting practice, not company-specific).
- Spinr today: not present — `financial_events.event_type` is a free-form enum used descriptively, not as an account reference (INFERRED from the schema description in `money-cra.md` §3.11).
- Benefit for Spinr at our scale: this is what `04-blueprint.md` H2's "Next" step (atomic header RPCs per settlement type) needs as a prerequisite design artifact, and it is exactly the item `04-blueprint.md` itself flags: "accountant for the chart of accounts (tax payable, promo expense, dispute reserve) before Next." Cost/effort: **S** (a design document + a lookup table), but it needs the accountant, not just engineering. Risk: low — additive.
- How it fails or gets gamed: an incomplete chart of accounts (missing "dispute reserve" or "tax payable") forces a later migration once the gap is found in production, which is exactly what CORP-001 (missing idempotency on the wind-down debit) and MONEY-009 (top-up disputes with no ledger row) already show happening informally.

### 2.3 Daily reconciliation against Stripe

### Reconcile against Stripe's own Payout/Balance report types, keyed on settlement, not creation — ADOPT
